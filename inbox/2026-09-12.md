## The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry

The author cites Corrie ten Boom, who said that if the devil can't make you sin, he'll make you busy, since it cuts off your connection to God. I believe this to be true since whenever I go through periods when I forget myself and start working only, and only focusing on my career, I get apart from God, and that's when I start to feel most overwhelmed, anxious, stressed, angry, and out of control. I find that these are terrible periods of time in my life.

Hurry is a spiritual condition that corrodes love, attention and depth. Modern life is intentionally designed for speed. Busy is different than hurried. You cannot love God or people while being hurried. It's a character deformation. Silence and solitude as re-training attention. I believe this is something I really have to keep in mind, since by being hurried, by being immersed in an ideal lifestyle I see on Instagram, by keeping no space for God in my life, I become a person who lives with no depth, with no attention to details, with no attention to the simple things of life that bring joy, I start living in automatic. I'm not distracted by chaos. I participate in a system that monetizes my attention, and I cooperate with it. The book cites a cardiologist called Meyer Friedman who rose to fame for theorizing that type A people who are chronically angry and in a hurry are more prone to a heart attack. He defines hurry sickness as a continuous struggle and unremitting attempt to accomplish or achieve more and more things or participate in more and more events in less time. Well, I've been living like this for more than 5 years already, and I'm scared - not only for the potential physical problems, but because I've learned to work and live like this, and I don't know another way to do it. And the cause? Being apart from God, putting my value in people's opinion and my output. Hurry is a form of violence against myself. Hurry is not the disease; it's the symptom of running from pain and running towards validation. Hurry is my coping mechanism for identity regulation. I believe there's a constant threat I should respond to; the fear of illegitimacy, if I stop, I might find out I'm not good enough. I was made in the image of God, and that means that there's potential within me. Not only potential in the way of becoming successful, but also by living a life to flourish and thrive. However, I must accept my limitations: I'm not God and I'm mortal. And the interesting thing is that we find God's will for our lives through our limitations. Living the Christian way is not living Jesus's life; it is living as if Jesus would be living our own lives. Adapt it. Incorporating him into our lives. Think: what would he do in my shoes?

Solitude and boredom are necessary. Christ did it. He used to wake up early to pray: "Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up, left the house and went off to a solitary place [eremos], where he prayed." Well, to be honest, the times (very few) that I've woken up early and prayed, I've felt the difference, it's very clear. Imagine if that's an isolated event, how could that be if it's a habit? The author remarks that many times Jesus came out of the wilderness with all sorts of clarity about his identity and calling. He was in touch with God. He was in a place of emotional equilibrium and spiritual succor. How bad we are at giving these moments the importance they deserve. The Sabbath. The detox of the mundane world. I'm VERY deep inside into other people's life. I've had a period in my life where I had no social media, not a single one - I still don't have Facebook, and this comes since I was in college. But now, I can't quit Instagram? Why? Because I have a fake sense of motivation that comes from watching other people's lives. The gym, the fancy cafe, the nice car, the nice clothes, the watch, the body, the food. I mean, it's not wrong to pursue that. But, I went from motivation, to greed for another person's life and THE LOSS OF GRATITUDE, JOY, AND CONTENTMENT IN OUR OWN. Sabbath is how we prepare our minds and bodies for the moments that matter most.

Even God rested on the 7th day. Why don't we? He created this day for man. It's a gift from him to us. It's a holy day. It's a day to index our hearts toward grateful recognition of God's reality and goodness. There are studies that show that you can live longer by practicing Sabbath. Invest your money and time in things that matter, like your relationship with God and life in his kingdom. Where you put your resources is where you put your heart.

Our irrational self is what God calls the flesh.

Practice simplicity. It is some form of minimalism. There are some tips in the book for living a simpler life, but the ones that I feel closer to are: opting for few, but better quality things. Give things away. Live by a budget. Appreciate the creation. Appreciate simple pleasures.

Right in this moment, all of us have everything we need to live a happy and content life: we have access to the Father. Begin the day with prayer. Let that set your emotional equilibrium, and let scripture set your view of the world. Begin the day with that. Don't use the phone until you've done that. From the recommendations to slow down my life, I feel closer to: driving at the speed limit. Don't text and drive. Showing up 10 minutes earlier. TURN MY SMARTPHONE INTO A DUMB PHONE. Set time for emails. Set time limit for social media. Single task. Take vacations. Cook my own food. Walk slow. Praying.

3 simple goals: slow down, live around the practices of Jesus, live from a center of abiding (spiritually). Try to not miss the goodness of each day.

Our days of pain are the building blocks of our character. Our crucible of Christlikeness.

Make it your ambition to live a quiet life.

Try to keep your soul away in peace and quiet.
