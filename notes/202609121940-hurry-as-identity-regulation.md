When I treat hurry as a productivity problem, I miss what it's actually doing: regulating my identity. Underneath the packed calendar is a fear of illegitimacy — if I slow down, I might find out I'm not actually good enough. Hurry becomes a way to keep that question from ever getting asked. It's not neutral busyness; it's a coping mechanism, and coping mechanisms don't go away just because I name them — they go away when the underlying fear gets addressed directly.
