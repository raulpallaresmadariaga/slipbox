Cardiologist Meyer Friedman coined "hurry sickness" to describe a continuous, unremitting struggle to accomplish or acquire more — more achievements, more events — in less time. He linked this Type A pattern, marked by chronic anger and urgency, to higher rates of heart attack. It reframes hurry from a scheduling inconvenience into a diagnosable, physiologically costly condition.
